EXPERIMENT 01
ARGON2 (ARGON2ID)

Objective

After performing the experiment, the student will be able to:

  1. Explain why fast hashes (MD5, SHA-256) are unsuitable for password storage and how offline dictionary/rainbow-table attacks exploit their speed.
  2. Describe Argon2's memory-hard design: the BLAKE2b-based compression function G, 1024-byte blocks, lanes, slices and passes, and data-dependent vs data-independent reference addressing.
  3. Configure the three cost parameters — memory cost m, time cost t, parallelism p — and reason about the security/usability trade-off of each.
  4. Demonstrate the role of the salt by comparing salted and unsalted hashing against a simulated rainbow-table attack.
  5. Benchmark real in-browser hashing times for Argon2id vs PBKDF2 and select parameters that hit a target time budget (e.g. OWASP's interactive-login guidance).

These outcomes map to Theory §1–6, the steps in the Procedure, and the exercises in the Simulation and Assignment.
